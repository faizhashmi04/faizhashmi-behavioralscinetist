Bachat Mitra

Mapping the behavioral drivers of digital financial trust among rural women — building a predictive trust model that operationalized behavioral signals into product design decisions at scale

↗ Trust model adopted as primary design rubric · Behavioral insights shaped product strategy for 50K+ users across 3 states

Overview

Bachat Mitra — "Savings Friend" — was a digital savings app designed for 10 million+ rural women in India's National Rural Livelihood Mission self-help groups. The behavioral challenge was fundamental: how do you measure and engineer trust in a digital financial product among populations where trust is mediated by social networks, ritual behavior, and lived experience — not interface features? This study treated trust as a behavioral construct to be measured, modeled, and operationalized rather than assumed.

Stakeholders & Collaboration

Coordinated with NRLM program officers, state-level SHG coordinators, and digital product teams across 3 states. Delivered quarterly behavioral insight briefings to funders and iteratively aligned findings with product sprint cycles to close the behavior-design gap in real time.

Behavioral Research Approach

  1. Conducted 60+ structured interviews and diary-style observations to map existing savings rituals, social trust networks, and specific behavioral barriers to digital adoption — identifying the beliefs and heuristics governing financial decisions.
  2. Designed Qualtrics surveys with adaptive branching logic (N=600+) to operationalize trust as a measurable behavioral construct — segmenting users by digital readiness, social proof sensitivity, and transparency orientation.
  3. Ran A/B behavioral tests on interface prototypes varying onboarding framing, social proof signals, and voice-guided features — measuring adoption intent and behavioral willingness-to-engage.
  4. Built a behavioral trust model linking interface attributes to adoption likelihood via multilevel regression across 3 regional cohorts — producing a predictive tool adopted as the product team's primary evaluation rubric.

Behavioral Impact & Outcomes

  • Behavioral trust measurement framework adopted as the product team's primary design evaluation rubric — replacing subjective design intuition with empirically grounded behavioral criteria
  • Voice-guided flows — identified as behaviorally superior through A/B testing — outperformed text-only conditions by 34% on task completion among low-literacy users
  • Behavioral segmentation model (3 distinct trust profiles) used to prioritize feature rollout sequences in pilot districts, reaching 50K+ potential users
  • Behavioral insights and policy recommendations presented to state government stakeholders, influencing digital financial inclusion strategy across Bihar and UP

EkaScribe AI

Diagnosing why India's semi-urban clinicians abandoned an AI scribe that promised to save them 12 hours a week — revealing a behavioral context-fit failure hidden beneath surface-level usability metrics

Contextual Inquiry & Shadowing Diary Study (WhatsApp) Post-Onboarding Survey Usage Analytics Behavioral Journey Mapping

↗ 23% mid-session abandonment reduction · 4/6 recommendations in EkaScribe 2.0 roadmap · Regional NLP for 60M+ users unlocked

🩺
02
Read Study ↓

Overview

Eka Care's EkaScribe uses ambient listening to convert clinician speech into structured clinical notes in real time — promising to save physicians 12+ hours of documentation per week. By 2024, EkaScribe had reached 1 million sessions, but a troubling behavioral signal had emerged: 67% of new users in Tier 2/3 Indian cities stopped active use within 14 days of onboarding — a 2.3× higher drop-off than metro counterparts with equivalent digital literacy. And 73% still had the app installed, indicating behavioral disengagement rather than rejection. The core behavioral science question: why do clinicians who express positive attitudes toward a tool consistently fail to sustain its use?

Stakeholders & Collaboration

Delivered findings to Eka Care's product, growth, and engineering leadership in a full-day readout with behavioral journey maps, video clips from diary study entries, and a tiered recommendation roadmap. Four of six recommendations entered the EkaScribe 2.0 product roadmap within six weeks. Diary study protocol adopted as a standard methodology across Eka's research playbook.

Behavioral Research Approach

  1. Contextual inquiry and live consultation shadowing across 8 physicians in 4 Indian states — mapping the actual behavioral choreography of the doctor-patient encounter to identify how EkaScribe's intervention points clashed with the rhythms, social dynamics, and cognitive demands of Tier 2/3 clinical practice.
  2. 3-week diary study via WhatsApp voice notes (n=12 clinicians) capturing emotional states, specific behavioral failure moments, and real-time workarounds at end-of-day — chosen over written diaries to reduce reporting burden and preserve emotional texture in participants' own language.
  3. Post-onboarding survey (n=340, metro vs. non-metro cohorts) measuring perceived usefulness, social friction, language barriers, and trust in AI accuracy as behavioral constructs; multilevel regression identified behavioral predictors of continued use — trust in AI accuracy emerged as the strongest predictor (β=0.71, p<0.001).
  4. Session-level usage analytics across 1,200 clinicians (drop-off triggers, feature engagement sequences, abandonment timestamps by city tier, specialty, and session age) — combined with task analysis decomposing the consultation into 18 discrete behavioral moments to precisely locate EkaScribe's friction zones.

Key Behavioral Findings

  • Social barrier: Patients in Tier 2/3 clinics interpreted AI scribe activation as doctor inattention — "I came to see you, not a computer" — making social perception of the technology the single most-cited discontinuation reason (58% of lapsed non-metro users vs. 14% metro)
  • Language barrier: Consultations in Bihar, UP, and Chhattisgarh unfold in Bhojpuri, Maithili, and Awadhi — not English. The cognitive cost of real-time dialect-to-clinical-English translation made the tool behaviorally unworkable; session length in non-metro clinics was 40% shorter, with most sessions ending prematurely at the medication entry step
  • Cognitive space: The deliberative pause between examination and prescription — which EkaScribe was designed to eliminate — was when physicians did their most critical diagnostic thinking. Ambient pressure to speak continuously collapsed this behavioral need; three physicians used the same phrase: "sochne ka waqt nahin mila" (I didn't get time to think)
  • Trust collapse: A single AI medication error triggered complete discontinuation in 71% of lapsed users — clinical contexts have near-zero error tolerance; the product had no behavioral recovery mechanism after the collapse event
  • Symbolic ritual: In Tier 2/3 contexts, handwriting a prescription functions as a behavioral signal of attentiveness and medical authority — the instant automated output inadvertently stripped this trust-generating ritual from the clinical encounter

Behavioral Impact & Outcomes

  • 23% reduction in mid-session abandonment after implementing patient-facing transparency cue in 3-clinic pilot (n=480 sessions, 8 weeks) — smallest behavioral intervention, largest immediate impact
  • Language barrier finding unlocked engineering resourcing for Bhojpuri, Maithili, and Awadhi NLP — covering 60M+ people in Eka's highest-growth markets
  • 4/6 recommendations adopted into EkaScribe 2.0 roadmap within 6 weeks: confidence scoring, deliberative buffer, specialty vocabulary templates, and patient transparency moment
  • Research reframed the team's model of Tier 2/3 adoption — retiring the "digital literacy gap" frame in favor of "contextual behavioral fit," embedding UXR as a pre-launch requirement for non-metro rollouts

AmTrue India

Read Study ↓

Overview

AmTrue India sells biodegradable menstrual cups online with a mission to shift Indian women from disposable products to reusable care. Traffic was healthy, ratings were strong — yet Q-Cup conversion sat below 2% and cart abandonment hit 78%. Standard analytics couldn't explain why. The behavioral hypothesis was that the purchase journey was saturated with unaddressed psychological barriers: body anxiety, regret aversion, and stigma-driven decision avoidance. Identifying and measuring these behavioral drivers — not surface-level UX friction — was the core research challenge.

Stakeholders & Collaboration

Commissioned by co-founders Ashish and Ichha Wanjari; biweekly behavioral insight briefings delivered directly to the founding team with interim readouts guiding live storefront iterations. Behavioral profile framework and survey instrument handed off as repeatable internal research assets, with two team members trained in ongoing independent behavioral data collection.

Behavioral Research Approach

  1. Behavioral analytics audit of 4 months of session data, scroll-depth heatmaps, and click-path logs — locating 63% of behavioral abandonment on the product detail page, before add-to-cart, consistent with trust-and-comprehension failure rather than checkout friction.
  2. 24 moderated behavioral observation sessions (RITE methodology) with women aged 18–38 across Tier 1 and 2 cities — think-aloud protocol mapping hesitation moments, avoidance behaviors, and decision-making schemas in real time.
  3. Qualtrics XM survey (N=320) with adaptive branching measuring product comprehension, brand trust, body anxiety, and price sensitivity as behavioral constructs; multilevel regression (R/lme4) isolated the unique behavioral predictors of purchase intent.
  4. K-means cluster analysis synthesized with observational patterns in NVivo produced 3 stable behavioral profiles — Body Anxious, Eco-Rational, and First-Timer Overwhelmed — each with distinct behavioral drivers requiring tailored intervention designs.

Behavioral Impact & Outcomes

  • Diagnosed that 63% of abandonment was a trust and behavioral comprehension problem occurring before add-to-cart — not a checkout friction problem — redirecting the entire intervention strategy
  • Identified regret aversion — not price sensitivity — as the primary behavioral barrier; risk-mitigation framing moved conversion more than further discounting at ₹499
  • Social proof dramatically outperformed clinical endorsement for the "Body Anxious" behavioral profile; peer video testimonials converted at 2.3× the rate of brand copy alone
  • 41% cart abandonment reduction (78% → 46%) following trust-signal elevation and mobile redesign informed by behavioral profile insights
  • 2.8× Q-Cup conversion lift; profile-matched campaigns convert at 1.9× unmatched traffic in ongoing A/B experiments — validating behavioral targeting as an ongoing data product

Cultural Ecology of Health

Overview

This dissertation investigated a central paradox in global health: despite significant national progress, stark subnational disparities in maternal and child health behaviors persist. The core behavioral science question — why do communities with similar access to care exhibit radically different health behaviors? — demanded a new explanatory framework. The Cultural Ecology of Health (CEH) model was developed to capture the multi-level behavioral drivers that standard epidemiological models miss, integrating community health worker behavior, traditional knowledge systems, and structural determinants as co-predictors of health behavior change.

Stakeholders & Collaboration

Engaged WHO program advisors, USAID health officials, and State Health Society Bihar. Coordinated with a cross-disciplinary team spanning public health, psychology, and anthropology. Framework findings contributed to WHO guideline discussions on community health worker integration and were cited in USAID South Asia programming.

Behavioral Research Approach

  1. 12+ months of embedded ethnographic observation across rural Bihar — mapping behavioral decision-making in situ across health facilities, traditional healing contexts, and household settings to identify unmeasured behavioral determinants of perinatal care.
  2. Study 1 applied multilevel behavioral modeling to test how ASHAs' own lived maternal health experiences predicted behavioral adoption patterns in their clients — revealing a behavioral transmission mechanism invisible to aggregate data.
  3. Study 2 triangulated interview analysis with survey data (N=820) to quantify how cultural beliefs and social norms shape antenatal care behavior — measuring the relative weight of attitudinal vs. structural behavioral drivers.
  4. Study 3 synthesized findings into the CEH comparative framework across 3 districts, identifying community-level behavioral moderators of health worker effectiveness and producing actionable predictions for program design.

Behavioral Impact & Outcomes

  • Three peer-reviewed publications in Frontiers journals — directly cited in WHO guideline discussions on community health worker behavioral integration strategies
  • Cultural Ecology of Health framework adopted as a teaching and analytical resource in two graduate public health programs — operationalizing behavioral science theory in health systems contexts
  • Framework influenced USAID programming decisions on traditional birth attendant integration in South Asia — translating behavioral evidence into policy at scale
  • Identified 3 previously unmeasured behavioral drivers of antenatal care refusal, reshaping the behavioral change communication strategy for program partners

Overview

A two-year Gates Foundation-funded behavioral intervention research initiative with Dalberg Design and Project Concern International India. The program challenged a foundational assumption embedded in existing behavioral models: that men in rural India are disengaged from reproductive and maternal health. Through deep behavioral observation and co-creation, we discovered rich — but systematically suppressed — male motivation. The research challenge was to map the behavioral mechanisms suppressing this motivation and design an intervention ecosystem that activated it.

Stakeholders & Collaboration

Led behavioral research synthesis; delivered monthly evidence briefings to Gates Foundation, translating behavioral findings into real-time program design decisions.

Behavioral Research Approach

  3. Behavioral journey mapping across 40+ households revealed 3 distinct male engagement typologies (Protector, Provider, Bystander) — each with different behavioral trigger points, motivational structures, and norm-based barriers requiring tailored behavioral nudges.
  4. Rapid behavioral prototype testing of 5 intervention concepts across 3 iterations (200+ community members) — measuring behavioral intent, social acceptability, and observed behavior change to validate and refine before deployment.

ASHA & Dai Behavioral Systems

Revealing how frontline health workers navigate two competing knowledge systems — and modeling the behavioral complementarity that transforms a perceived conflict into a scalable care ecosystem

Overview

India's health system invested $3.6 billion deploying 1 million ASHAs while simultaneously marginalizing Dais — traditional birth attendants who hold deep community trust and behavioral influence accumulated over centuries. Rather than treating this as an ideological conflict, this study applied behavioral systems thinking to model how each actor's behavioral repertoire, belief system, and role identity functioned — revealing a sophisticated ecosystem of complementary behavioral expertise that existing policy had failed to map.

Stakeholders & Collaboration

Collaborated with Bihar State Health Society, WHO South-East Asia regional advisors, and USAID India health program teams. Behavioral framework findings were incorporated into stakeholder briefings that directly shaped policy recommendations on traditional provider integration across two national programs.

Behavioral Research Approach

  1. 75 in-depth behavioral interviews (45 ASHAs, 30 Dais) across rural Bihar, using grounded theory to build an empirical model of how each group constructs role identity, defines expertise boundaries, and navigates behavioral decision-making in ambiguous care situations.
  2. Developed a comparative behavioral framework across 6 dimensions — revealing systematic behavioral complementarity rather than conflict: ASHAs and Dais activate different behavioral systems for different phases of the perinatal journey.
  3. Triangulated interview analysis with 3 months of direct behavioral observation across 12 health facilities, validating the framework through member-checking with 15 participants to confirm behavioral accuracy.
  4. Synthesized behavioral findings with a multidisciplinary team to produce integration recommendations grounded in how actual behavioral complementarity could be operationalized in policy and training design.

Behavioral Impact & Outcomes

  • Published in Frontiers in Health Services — cited in USAID and WHO programming documents on traditional provider roles and behavioral integration strategies
  • Behavioral complementarity framework reframed the policy conversation from "replacing Dais" to "designing for collaborative behavioral ecosystems" — shifting the design problem entirely
  • Research design adopted as a methodological model for 2 follow-on studies on behavioral systems in pluralistic health delivery in Sub-Saharan Africa
  • Informed ASHA capacity-building training modules across 4 districts, improving inter-provider behavioral collaboration metrics
